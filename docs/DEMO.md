# Demo and pitch

0:00–0:20: Preserved lock landing. Problem: downstream AI publishing tools need approval of exact content.

0:20–1:10: Review a harmless prepared AI draft and approve using a physical passkey. Show actual Monad transaction and receipt. Explain that P256 verifies this content commitment, not merely earlier login.

1:10–1:50: Verify from another application with an independently trusted approver. Change a word: publishing blocked.

1:50–2:20: Revoke with a fresh physical passkey; verify again: revoked. Already disclosed content cannot be recalled.

2:20–3:00: Show a linked revision needing new consent and the compact SDK. Contract/SDK are reusable infrastructure; our pages are examples. State testnet and device limits honestly.

Do not record .env or keys. Never describe synthetic assertions as Windows Hello. Prepare sponsor and passkey before recording. The prepared draft is not a live model API call.

## Pitch — maximum two minutes

Introduce the actual team. AI tools often keep approvals as platform-local mutable flags that cannot travel with an exact content version. Orvena supplies passkey-authorized, revocable approvals on Monad. Other applications detect changes and revocation without relying on the original platform database. Drafts are not uploaded to the chain.

Initial customers: AI support-response and company-publication tool developers. Their own systems decide organizational authority; our SDK supplies cryptographic consent, replay protection and revocation. Possible services: managed sponsorship and integration support, preserving open verification.

Evidence: live Monad contract, tested SDK/sponsor, real testnet approve/revise/revoke, user-confirmed Windows device passkey approval/revocation on the final HTTPS origin, and our separate-origin publisher. Claim independent interest only if obtained. Next: three targeted developer pilots, sponsor operations and security review.
